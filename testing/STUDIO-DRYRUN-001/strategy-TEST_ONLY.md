## Concept A — Evidence puzzle / mystery: The Missing Medal

**Creative hypothesis:** A community contest winner finds their medal replaced by a replica. Three small inconsistencies reveal that a trusted organizer swapped it; the winner recovers the original through a fair public evidence reveal, providing nonviolent revenge.

- **Hook hypothesis, 0–3 seconds:** Lead with the contradiction that the winner's medal bears the wrong date. Offer one concrete puzzle rather than explain the whole contest.
- **Structure hypothesis, 60 seconds:** 0–3 contradiction; 3–12 ownership and stakes; 12–26 two clues; 26–38 plausible wrong explanation; 38–50 final clue exposes the swap; 50–57 recovery and proportionate consequence; 57–60 callback to the date. These allocations sum to 60 seconds.
- **Duration hypothesis:** 60 seconds within the supplied range may leave enough space for a fair clue trail and an understandable payoff without a second subplot.
- **Caption hypothesis:** Speech-synchronized short phrases, stable high-contrast placement clear of platform controls, selective emphasis on the date and object; no separate paragraph viewers must read while hearing new narration. Exact placement needs later device checks.
- **Retention hypothesis:** A visible contradiction may create a question viewers want answered; the callback may invite rewatching to inspect clues. Neither outcome is verified.
- **Risks/limitations:** Unfairly withheld information could make the reveal arbitrary; too many names could obscure causality; busy gameplay could mask a clue. Keep characters few and ensure all essential evidence is spoken. No actual viewer comprehension test exists.
- **Measurement proposal:** See the unexecuted Concept A design in measurement sections P1.1–P1.4 below: hook contrast, conditional primary continuation proportion, prospective eligibility/sufficiency gates and inconclusive rules. No baseline or target is established.

## Concept B — Social betrayal / revenge drama: The Stolen Thank-You

**Creative hypothesis:** A teammate secretly claims credit for a shared community build. A missing attribution becomes the mystery; an original draft and a witness resolve it. The protagonist corrects the record rather than humiliates or harasses the teammate.

- **Hook hypothesis, 0–3 seconds:** Open on a thank-you speech crediting everyone except the person who made the design.
- **Structure hypothesis, 65 seconds:** 0–3 social slight; 3–15 relationship and contribution; 15–29 missing credit and denial; 29–42 search for original draft; 42–54 witness connects the clues; 54–62 calm public correction; 62–65 relational consequence. Total 65 seconds.
- **Duration hypothesis:** 65 seconds may allow the relationship and motive to remain understandable alongside the evidence, at the cost of a longer setup.
- **Caption hypothesis:** Synchronize phrase-sized captions to narration; use names or role labels in spoken text instead of color alone to distinguish people; emphasize the credit reversal sparingly.
- **Retention hypothesis:** An intelligible unfairness may create interest in a proportionate resolution; escalating evidence may sustain that question. No audience preference is known.
- **Risks/limitations:** A generic betrayal could feel predictable; prolonged grievance could delay the mystery. Avoid identifiable real people, copied anecdotes, harassment and an unearned witness intervention; establish the witness earlier.
- **Measurement proposal:** In a separately authorized future test, inspect early retention, average view duration, completion and the drop around the evidence search. Engagement themes might indicate perceived fairness, but cannot establish broad audience demand. Baselines, sample sizes and metric access are unknown.

## Concept C — Comic caper / mystery-revenge: The Decoy Trophy

**Creative hypothesis:** A prankster repeatedly hides a club trophy. The protagonist leaves a harmless decoy that makes a distinctive squeak; the sound exposes the culprit, leading to a playful return-the-trophy consequence.

- **Hook hypothesis, 0–3 seconds:** Present the odd question of why a supposedly silent trophy squeaked inside someone's bag.
- **Structure hypothesis, 50 seconds:** 0–3 comic anomaly; 3–11 pattern of disappearances; 11–23 decoy setup; 23–35 misdirection and squeak; 35–44 culprit explanation and trophy recovery; 44–50 joke callback. Total 50 seconds.
- **Duration hypothesis:** 50 seconds may suit a single simple mechanism and avoid stretching the joke. It offers less space for a layered mystery.
- **Caption hypothesis:** Synchronize readable phrases, briefly emphasize the narrated squeak cue, and ensure sound-dependent meaning is also intelligible in captions. Avoid flashing effects or unreadably fast punchline text.
- **Retention hypothesis:** Comic incongruity may attract curiosity and a short setup/payoff may support completion; humor may fail for an unknown audience. No performance expectation is established.
- **Risks/limitations:** A sound cue could be confusing with narration or music; explaining the decoy too late could feel like cheating. Establish the mechanism before the reveal; keep the prank harmless and avoid imitation of dangerous traps.
- **Measurement proposal:** Under future separate authorization, inspect early retention, completion, average view duration and any available replay indicator around the reveal. Comments might clarify whether the mechanism was understood, not prove comedic appeal. Metric availability and comparison baselines remain unknown.
