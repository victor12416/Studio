## Research evidence — Part 6 of 6

• H1: A first-line unresolved conflict might attract more initial attention than generic “storytime” framing when the footage is identical.
• H2: Mystery/horror and family/revenge could produce different completion profiles, but only controlled equal-channel tests can reveal this.
• H3: Mid-length 45–75-second stories may balance plot payoff and scroll friction, but this sample *cannot substantiate* the optimum.
• H4: Truly original story scripts plus self-captured, consistently moving parkour gameplay may lower copyright/reuse risk and distinguish from mass-produced formats; monetization outcomes *not guaranteed*.

Research limitations and Reviewer check list
1. *Assignment shortfall:* 0 / 30 fully confirmed composite-format Shorts; 9 candidates from prior dates, no confirmed candidate in Oct 15–Nov 14; *30 recent adjacent Shorts are not replacements* for 30 niche matches.
2. YouTube direct playback inaccessible; no actual content inspection, full transcripts, captions, exact spoken hook, voiceover/cut pacing, creator licensed use, or verified duration for A. A candidates may include long-form or non-Short uploads despite labels.
3. A engagement/date figures obtained from search index; as-of timestamp unknown and may be very stale. B–E public tracker counts reflect individual third-party site snapshots, not independently rechecked live on YouTube.
4. Sampling is *convenience/availability based*, with large-channel selection bias and older low-reach direct leads. Do not compare category winners, causality, retention or views/day without age/channel-normalized data.
5. Classification by titles is tentative; some “horror”/“revenge” titles may not match the actual narrative. No true audience demographic data available.
